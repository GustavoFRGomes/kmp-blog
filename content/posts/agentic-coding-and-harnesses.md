# Agentic Coding and Harnesses
I have been using pi.dev quite a lot lately and with it I am pairing my [OpenRouter](openrouter.org) key and setup. The latter was something someone recommended to me on Bluesky, I must admit at first I thought it was not useful, but slowly I began appreciating it not just for the amount of models but also for the opportunities to test out new unreleased, or just released, models.

My pi.dev setup is quite small, but does have some core extensions:
* bigpowers
* gentle-pi
* pi-caveman
* pi-ponytail
* pi-web-access
These have helped me tremendously, even though I was also not a huge fan of gentle-pi in the beginning because of it's size and overhead. This and all the others have culminated in a very nice fruitful way of performing my personal forays into "agentic coding", I know the keyword term is "agentic engineering" but I do use it mainly for coding, the engineering part I do try to make it beforehand or manually specially because it's the fun part. I do let myself be much more lazy when it comes to implementing and writing out boilerplate code, things such as serialisation code, connecting DI components together after I set up DI and other things that fall out of the "architecting" or novelty aspect of coding.

I do love to code, I like to beat my brain with harder things but to be really fair there are a lot of things that I do prefer to only review rather than also write tediously. What I have also done is solve engineering problem by engineering problem, in a cycle, and in between (while I learn and try to figure out the next solution) I will let the agent harvest the power of my pi harness to do the proverbial "plumbing".

For work, I must admit that I am more hands-on, the main reason being the stakes involved. I could do more or less the same process but the main reason I avoid it, has to do with the fact that to me it's negligent to not even look at the plumbing, there is also much more complexity and I do genuinely care about the overall health of the entire codebase, not just the parts that I work on (which is a small sliver of the overall application codebase). Therefore I am much more free on my own pet-projects which also lets me explore a bit more of the world out there. It also enables me to explore different models, understand my harness and the process of making plugins and what is involved in a coding-agent + harness, which at work sadly I am stuck with Copilot license that does not let me use pi.dev with it.

## Exploration is Fun
There are many models that have different strengths and weaknesses, and the more you explore and experiment the better. Of course the opposite is also true, if you truly use the same model for months on end you get to know how to steer it much better, but the problem with that approach (at least to me) is that we will become very much blinded for potentially better models that have come out, specially ones from different providers.

So do not be afraid to just try things out. I do not think there is a single model that we can do everything with, even though the more "frontier" models try to actually be the all-encompassing solution, models such as (at the time of writing) Fable 5, GPT-5.6 Sol/Terra, DeepSeek Pro.
